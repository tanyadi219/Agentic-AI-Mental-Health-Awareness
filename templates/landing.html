Mental health support shouldn't have barriers

Around the world, millions of people struggle in silence — not because they don't want help, but because help isn't accessible, affordable, or available in their language. MindBridge was built to change that.

Pain doesn't speak only English

Listens first, advises second

All World Languages

Write in any language — Hindi, Arabic, Spanish, Swahili, Japanese, Korean, and 40+ more. MindBridge detects your language and replies in it. No setup.

Crisis Detection — Before the AI Responds

A multilingual keyword scanner runs on every message before the AI sees it. For high-risk messages, crisis hotlines surface immediately — safety cannot be bypassed by the AI.

😊

Mood Check-In

Each session begins with a simple mood check-in — a 1–10 slider. Your score is shared with MindBridge to help it respond with the right depth of care.

💨

Guided Breathing Exercise

The built-in breathing timer guides you through box breathing — the same technique used by therapists and first responders to calm an overwhelmed nervous system.

📚

Real Mental Health Education

Not just platitudes — real, honest explanations of depression, anxiety, grief, and crisis. Including what to say (and not to say) when supporting someone you love.

🔒

Private by Design

About MindBridge

MindBridge is an open-source Agentic AI project built to make mental health support more accessible globally. It uses the openai/gpt-oss-120b language model via Groq's ultra-fast inference API.

The AI is guided by a carefully crafted system prompt built on principles from Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT), Motivational Interviewing, and evidence-based suicide prevention frameworks — designed to listen first, validate always, and guide gently toward professional help.

The project uses a 5-layer defence-in-depth safety architecture — ensuring crisis resources reach users regardless of AI behaviour, every time.

⚠️ Disclaimer: MindBridge is not a licensed therapist or medical professional. It cannot diagnose or treat any condition. Always consult a qualified mental health professional for diagnosis or treatment. In a crisis, call 988 or go to your nearest emergency room immediately.
