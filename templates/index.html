💨 Breathing Exercise

Used by therapists and first responders. A few minutes can genuinely calm your nervous system.

Press Start

Box breathing: 4 seconds in — 4 hold — 4 out — 4 hold. Repeat 5 times.

It's okay if your mind wanders. Just gently come back to the breath each time.

🧠 Why it works

Box breathing activates the parasympathetic nervous system — the body's natural "rest and calm" response. It reduces cortisol, slows heart rate, and interrupts the anxiety cycle.

🌿 When to use it

  • When you feel a panic attack starting
  • Before a difficult conversation
  • When overwhelmed and can't focus
  • Before sleep if your mind won't quiet
  • Any time emotions feel too big

💬 Talk about it

If breathing exercises aren't enough, that's okay — sometimes the weight is too heavy to carry alone. Talk to MindBridge about what you're going through.

5-4-3-2-1 Grounding

When panic or distress peaks, this technique pulls you back into the present moment:

  • 👁️ Name 5 things you can see right now
  • ✋ Notice 4 things you can physically touch
  • 👂 Identify 3 things you can hear
  • 👃 Find 2 things you can smell
  • 👅 Notice 1 thing you can taste

This works because it redirects the brain's attention away from distress toward concrete reality.

Understanding Depression

Depression is an illness, not a character flaw or weakness. It changes how the brain processes thoughts — making everything feel heavier and more permanent than it actually is.

  • Depression lies — it tells you things will never change. They can and do.
  • You don't have to "cheer up" — you need support, not effort.
  • Many people recover with therapy, support, and time.
  • Reaching out is a sign of strength, not failure.
💛

Understanding Anxiety

Anxiety is the nervous system's alarm system — it was designed to protect you. But sometimes it gets stuck "on" even when there's no real danger.

  • Physical symptoms (racing heart, tight chest) are real — not imagined.
  • Avoiding things makes anxiety grow; gentle exposure shrinks it.
  • CBT (Cognitive Behavioural Therapy) has strong evidence for anxiety.
  • Breathing slowly genuinely helps — it signals safety to your brain.
🕊️

Understanding Grief

Grief has no correct timeline, no stages you must follow in order, and no way to "do it right." It is simply love with nowhere to go.

  • Grief can be for a person, a relationship, a future, or an identity.
  • You don't "get over" loss — you learn to carry it differently over time.
  • Some days will feel unbearable. That doesn't mean it's always going to be this way.
  • Let people sit with you. You don't need to explain or justify your grief.
📖

Recognising Suicide Warning Signs

Asking someone directly about suicide does not increase risk — it shows you care and opens a door.

  • What to watch for: talking about death or being a burden, giving away possessions, sudden calmness after depression, withdrawing from people, saying goodbye
  • Say: "I've noticed you seem really low lately. I care about you. Are you having thoughts of hurting yourself?"
  • Listen without judgment. Don't promise secrecy.
  • Stay with them and help connect them to professional support.
  • Remove access to means (medications, sharp objects) when safe to do so.

Daily Practices That Genuinely Help

Small, consistent actions can meaningfully shift mental health over time:

  • Sleep 7–9 hours consistently — sleep deprivation worsens every mental health condition
  • Move your body — even a 10-minute walk outside changes brain chemistry
  • Eat nourishing food and stay hydrated — gut health and mental health are linked
  • Connect with one person today — even a brief, genuine interaction helps
  • Limit doom-scrolling — curate what enters your mind
  • Write one sentence about how you feel — externalising emotions reduces their intensity
  • Spend time in natural light — it regulates mood and sleep rhythms
💜

For Friends & Family

Supporting someone in mental health distress can feel overwhelming. Here's what actually helps:

  • Listen more than you speak. Resist the urge to fix or give advice immediately.
  • Say: "I'm here. I'm not going anywhere. You don't have to explain."
  • Don't say: "It could be worse" / "Just be positive" / "You have so much to live for."
  • Check in again in a few days — one text can matter more than you know.
  • You can't force someone to get help. You can keep showing up.
  • Look after yourself too. Supporting someone struggling is emotionally demanding.

ℹ️ About MindBridge

What is MindBridge?

MindBridge is a compassionate AI companion built specifically to support people experiencing mental health challenges — including anxiety, depression, grief, loneliness, and crisis. It was designed with one principle: no one should feel alone when they're struggling.

It listens first, validates your experience, offers evidence-based coping tools, and connects you to professional support when you need it — all without judgment and available 24 hours a day, in every language.

🤖 How it works

MindBridge uses the openai/gpt-oss-120b open-source language model via the Groq inference API. Every message passes through a multi-layer safety system before and after the AI responds — ensuring crisis resources reach you even if the AI itself has a problem.

The AI is guided by a detailed system prompt trained on principles from Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT), Motivational Interviewing, and evidence-based suicide prevention frameworks.

MindBridge responds in all world languages — it detects your language automatically. No setup required.

🛡️ The 5-Layer Safety System

  • Layer 1: Keyword scanner — runs before the AI, in 15+ languages
  • Layer 2: Risk context injection — informs the AI of the severity level
  • Layer 3: System prompt crisis protocol — AI trained to respond safely
  • Layer 4: Crisis block prepend — hotlines always surfaced for high-risk messages
  • Layer 5: UI crisis banner & alert bar — visual escalation in the interface

⚠️ Important Disclaimer

MindBridge is NOT a substitute for professional mental health care. It is not a licensed therapist, psychologist, or medical professional and cannot diagnose or treat any condition.

It is designed for emotional support and psychoeducation — a companion, not a clinician.

If you are in crisis, call 988 (US) or go to your nearest emergency room immediately.

🛠 Technology Stack

  • AI Model: openai/gpt-oss-120b (open-source) via Groq API
  • Language Detection: Unicode script detection + LLM multilingual capability
  • Backend: Python 3 + Flask
  • Frontend: HTML5, CSS3, Vanilla JavaScript
  • Safety: Multilingual keyword crisis detection + LLM risk prompting
  • Privacy: No conversation history stored server-side
